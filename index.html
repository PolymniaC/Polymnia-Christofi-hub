SkyWest Strategy Proposal

Conducted regression analysis using airport level operational data from SkyWest Airlines to examine the key drivers of flight delays. Cleaned and prepared the dataset, identified a heavily right skewed distribution of delay minutes, and applied a log transformation to improve model validity by better meeting OLS assumptions. Estimated and interpreted a log-linear regression model with airport fixed effects to control for structural differences across airports. Used regression results and supporting visualisations to identify the most important operational drivers of delays and inform strategic recommendations, including standardised turnaround processes and targeted operational buffers to reduce delay propagation.

Predicting Customer Churn in the Telecommunications Industry Using Machine Learning

Conducted a machine learning analysis to predict customer churn using a real-world telecommunications dataset. Cleaned and prepared the data by removing invalid empty-string entries that caused preprocessing errors, converting data types, encoding categorical variables, and engineering new features. Performed exploratory data analysis using distributions, boxplots, correlation heatmaps, and churn segmentation to support modelling decisions. Trained and evaluated logistic regression and random forest classifiers for binary churn prediction, comparing performance using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC, and confusion matrices. Assessed multicollinearity using VIF to support model interpretation, and concluded that logistic regression was the stronger and more suitable predictive model for this churn classification task.

Machine Learning Predictive Model for News Popularity

The primary objective of this study was to develop a machine learning model that can accurately predict the popularity of online news articles. Our approach involved analyzing various elements of an article, including its content, emotional impact, and the timing of its publication, to determine how these factors collectively influence the likelihood of an article being shared. After cleaning the data and using various scaling techniques such as Min-Max Scaling, Robust Scaling, and Standard Scaling, we drew correlation heatmaps and boxplots to understand the effect of attributes like the day of the week on the number of shares, ultimately identifying key factors that influence article popularity. Finally, we concluded that the day of the week significantly impacted article popularity, with articles published on certain days (like weekends) demonstrating a higher likelihood of falling into the high engagement category.
